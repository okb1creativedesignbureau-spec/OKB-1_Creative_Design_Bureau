# Convair 880 with Anti-Shock Bodies — The Transitional Concept

Before the Convair 990 Coronado introduced its distinctive large anti-shock bodies (ASB), Convair engineers had already been studying methods to improve the transonic efficiency of the earlier Convair 880 airframe.

One plausible intermediate configuration would have been a **modified Convair 880-22 and 880-22M equipped with smaller anti-shock bodies**, while retaining the original **General Electric CJ805-3B turbojet engines**.

This configuration represents a transitional aerodynamic concept: improving cruise efficiency without the extensive redesign that eventually produced the Convair 990.

---

# Purpose of the Anti-Shock Bodies

At high subsonic cruise speeds, aircraft approaching the **transonic regime (Mach 0.8+)** experience localized shockwaves on the wing. These shocks increase drag and reduce aerodynamic efficiency.

Anti-shock bodies were developed to:

- Reduce transonic wave drag
- Delay shock formation along the wing
- Improve lift distribution across the span
- Increase cruise efficiency at high Mach numbers

Convair's aerodynamic research suggested that properly designed ASBs could allow the aircraft to **cruise faster without significant drag penalties**.

---

# Configuration — Modified CV-880-22 / CV-880-22M

In this concept, the base aircraft remains structurally identical to the production Convair 880 variants.

### Airframe
- **Fuselage length:** ~39.4 m (129 ft)
- **Wingspan:** 36.58 m (120 ft)
- **Wing airfoils:**
  - Root: NACA 64-0012
  - Tip: NACA 64-0008

### Engines
- **4 × General Electric CJ805-3B turbojets**
- Thrust: ~52 kN (11,700 lbf) each

Unlike the Convair 990, the engines remain **pure turbojets rather than aft-fan turbofans**.

---

# Anti-Shock Body Design

Instead of the large fuel-carrying bodies used on the Convair 990, this conceptual 880 modification uses **smaller aerodynamic fairings mounted on the wing trailing edge**.

### Differences Compared to the Convair 990

| Feature | 880-ASB Concept | Convair 990 |
|--------|----------------|-------------|
| ASB size | Small aerodynamic fairings | Large extended pods |
| Fuel storage | Minimal or none | Significant fuel volume |
| Purpose | Drag reduction | Drag reduction + fuel capacity |
| Structural change | Minimal | Significant wing modification |

These smaller ASBs would primarily act as **area-rule aerodynamic devices**, rather than structural fuel tanks.

---

# Performance Expectations

Adding ASBs to the Convair 880 could theoretically provide several aerodynamic improvements.

### Possible Benefits

- Reduced transonic drag
- Slightly improved cruise efficiency
- Increased maximum cruise Mach
- Lower fuel consumption at cruise

### Estimated Cruise Characteristics

| Parameter | Standard CV-880 | 880-ASB Concept |
|----------|----------------|----------------|
| Cruise Mach | ~0.82 | ~0.84 (estimated) |
| Range | ~4,900 km | Slightly improved |
| Fuel efficiency | Baseline | Moderate improvement |

These gains would come **without changing the propulsion system**, making it a relatively low-risk upgrade.

---

# Engineering Significance

The concept illustrates an important stage in Convair’s aerodynamic thinking.

Instead of immediately developing the much larger and more complex **Convair 990 Coronado**, engineers could have explored an intermediate step:

**An aerodynamically refined Convair 880 using anti-shock bodies.**

This approach would have:

- Preserved existing production tooling
- Reduced development cost
- Allowed aerodynamic testing before committing to a full redesign

---

# Relationship to the Convair 990

The Convair 990 ultimately expanded the ASB concept significantly.

While the 880-ASB concept uses small aerodynamic fairings, the 990 introduced:

- Much larger anti-shock bodies
- Additional fuel storage within the pods
- Modified wing structure
- CJ805-23 aft-fan turbofan engines

In many ways, the hypothetical **880-ASB configuration can be viewed as the aerodynamic stepping stone between the Convair 880 and the Convair 990 Coronado.**

---

# Why This Concept Matters for the Model 60 Project

Understanding this intermediate aerodynamic research helps explain how Convair engineering philosophy evolved.

The same aerodynamic platform that produced the **880 and 990** also forms the structural basis for the later **Model 60 twin-jet reconstruction**.

By studying these incremental improvements — such as the addition of anti-shock bodies — we gain insight into how Convair might have continued refining its airliner designs had it remained active in the commercial aviation market.
