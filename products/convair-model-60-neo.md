# Growth Potential — Turning a 4-Engine Wing into a 737/A320-Class Aircraft Family

One of the most surprising insights from the CAD reconstruction is that the **Convair Model 60 inherits a wing originally designed for a four-engine airliner** — now repurposed for a **shorter, lighter twin-engine narrow-body**.

This creates a **structural and aerodynamic growth margin far beyond typical narrow-body aircraft of its era**.

In practice, this places the Model 60 in a similar long-term evolution path as:

- **Boeing 737** (Original → Classic → NG → MAX)
- **Airbus A320** (A320 → A321 stretch family)

> **The Convair Model 60 could realistically evolve into a multi-generation aircraft family — if Convair had survived.**

# Engine Evolution — The Key to Stretching the Airframe

Before meaningful fuselage stretching becomes feasible, **engine modernization is the primary requirement**.

The original CJ805 turbofan family provided adequate thrust, but suffered from:

- High fuel burn  
- High noise  
- Limited thrust growth margin  

To expand payload, range, and fuselage length, the Model 60 must follow a propulsion evolution similar to Boeing and Airbus.

# First Stretch — CJ805-41C or JT8D-219  
### *(737-100 → 737-200 Class)*

### Objectives
- Maintain thrust-to-weight ratio (T/W)
- Increase passenger capacity
- Preserve center of gravity (CG) stability

---

## Engine Options

| Engine | Thrust |
|---|---|
| CJ805-41C *(speculative)* | **74.7 kN** (16,800 lbf) |
| Pratt & Whitney **JT8D-219** | **93 kN** (21,000 lbf) |

---

## Fuselage Stretch Strategy

To keep CG neutral:

- **+1 m forward of wing**
- **+1 m aft of wing**
- **Total stretch: +2 m**

This avoids major landing gear or tailplane redesign.

---

## Passenger Capacity Gain

Assuming **33 in (0.838 m) seat pitch** and **5-abreast (2+3)** layout:

| Added Length | Added Rows | Added Seats |
|---|---|---|
| +2 m | ~2–3 rows | ~10–15 seats |

This places the Model 60 in the **737-200 market class**, but with:

- A **wider cabin**
- Higher cruise speed potential
- Better aerodynamic margin

## Advantage Over the 737-300

Unlike the 737-300 — which required:

- Flattened nacelles  
- Reduced ground clearance  
- Compromised inlet geometry  

The **Model 60 inherits Convair 880 landing gear geometry**, enabling:

- **Full circular nacelles**
- **Higher ground clearance**
- Cleaner airflow and lower drag

## CG & Nacelle Placement Adjustments

To balance heavier modern engines:

- Engines moved **slightly forward**
- Nacelles mounted **higher**
- CG corrected via fuselage plugs:

# Structural & Aerodynamic Consequences of Stretching

Each stretch increases:

- Maximum Takeoff Weight (MTOW)  
- Fuel capacity  
- Wing bending loads  
- Structural reinforcement needs  

<p>
    <br>
    <center>

    align="center"

However, because the wing originates from a **larger quad-jet**, it provides:

- High structural margin  
- Large lift reserve  
- Tolerance for payload expansion  

> **This suggests the Model 60 could scale without requiring a new wing — unlike many competitors.**

# Key Insight — Convair Accidentally Designed a Future-Proof Narrow-Body Platform

The CAD reconstruction reveals a striking conclusion:

> **By inheriting a large high-speed wing from a quad-engine airliner, the Convair Model 60 unintentionally became an ideal stretch platform.**

Had Convair survived, the Model 60 could realistically have evolved into:

- A **737-class multi-generation family**
- A **direct A320/A321 competitor**
- A **third dominant narrow-body lineage**
