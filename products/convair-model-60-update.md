

# Transitional Variants — The JT8D Era and Incremental Growth Strategy

Before entering the high-bypass turbofan generation (CFM56 / V2500), the Convair Model 60 would logically pass through a **JT8D-powered transitional phase**, mirroring how the 737-100 evolved into the 737-200 and later Classic series.

This phase represents the **bridge between early CJ805-powered jets and fully modern narrow-body configurations**.

---

# Model 60-100 — Baseline (CJ805 Era)

**Length:** ~34 m (111.5 ft)  
**Engines:**  
- CJ805-23B (~71 kN / 16,000 lbf)  
- Optional CJ805-41C (~74.7 kN / 16,800 lbf)

This version establishes:
- 5-abreast cabin (2+3)
- 33 in seat pitch baseline
- MTOW ~60,300 kg (132,965 lb)
- T/W ≈ 0.24

Comparable market role: early 737-100 / 737-200 territory.

---

# Model 60-200 — First Stretch (Mirroring 737-200 Philosophy)

## Structural Change
- +1 m forward of wing  
- +1 m aft of wing  
- Total stretch: +2 m  
- New length: ~36 m (118 ft)

CG remains largely unchanged because the plugs are balanced fore and aft of the wing box.

## Engine Options
- CJ805-41C (upper limit of CJ evolution)
- Pratt & Whitney JT8D series  
  - Most realistic: JT8D-15 to JT8D-219  
  - JT8D-219: ~93 kN (21,000 lbf)

The introduction of the JT8D improves:
- Fuel efficiency
- Reliability
- Global service support
- Airline acceptance

### Capacity Gain
With +2 m cabin:
- ~2–3 additional seat rows
- +10 to +15 passengers
- Estimated capacity: ~105–120 passengers

At this stage, the Model 60 becomes a serious 737-200-class competitor.

---

# Model 60-250 — Intermediate Stretch (Pre-High-Bypass Bridge)

## Additional Stretch
- +0.5 m forward of wing root  
- +0.5 m aft of wing root  
- Total additional: +1 m  
- Overall length: ~37 m (121.4 ft)

This creates a bridge configuration between the early narrow-body twinjet and a fully modernized high-bypass variant.

---

## Engine Rationalization

At ~37 m fuselage length:

### CJ805 Option Becomes Marginal
- Higher OEW
- Higher MTOW
- Increased wing loading
- Reduced takeoff margin

### JT8D-219 Becomes the Only Sensible Option

**JT8D-219 (~93 kN)** provides:
- Adequate thrust reserve
- Improved climb gradient
- Better payload-range capability
- Acceptable T/W even with higher MTOW

At this point, the family begins consolidating around JT8D as the primary engine.

---

# Performance Implications of the 60-250 (JT8D-219 Only)

Assuming increased MTOW:

| Parameter | Estimated |
|-----------|-----------|
| MTOW | ~65,000–68,000 kg |
| Total thrust | 2 × 93 kN |
| T/W ratio | ~0.27–0.28 |

This is stronger than the original CJ805 configuration, giving:

- Better takeoff field performance  
- Higher climb rate  
- More fuel carriage  
- Increased range  

It transforms the aircraft from “adequate” into competitive.

---

# Architectural Advantage Over 737-200

Unlike the 737-200, the Model 60 still benefits from:

- Larger wing area (inherited from quad-jet)
- Higher aspect ratio
- Higher cruise Mach potential
- Greater lift margin for stretch growth

This means the Model 60-250 could stretch further without wing redesign.

---

# Strategic Evolution Path

1. **Model 60-100** — CJ805 baseline  
2. **Model 60-200** — +2 m stretch, CJ805 or JT8D option  
3. **Model 60-250** — +1 m additional stretch, JT8D-219 only  
4. **Model 60-300 series** — CFM56-3 re-engine  
5. **Model 60-400 series** — IAE V2500 and major stretch  

---

# Engineering Insight — Controlled Growth vs Radical Redesign

The JT8D transitional phase demonstrates an important principle:

> The Model 60 does not need a new wing to grow — it only needs progressively better engines.

Because the platform originated from a larger four-engine airliner, it contains latent performance capacity that becomes unlocked as engine technology improves.

This makes the aircraft evolution believable, economically rational, and structurally coherent.
